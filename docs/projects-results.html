Projects: built, and tested until they pass

What the proposal described is built: projects read from requests, meetings and session write-ups, plans agreed with you, status changes in your own words, and questions answered from the graph. This page is what was built, how it was tested, and what the tests found.

Everything in the proposal's nine steps is in the repo, and 177 unit tests pass (45 on the change sets and cases, 44 on the graph, the answers and the scripts). Nothing is committed.

Reading inputs into projects: across seven worked cases, three runs each, Sonnet 5 proposed the right changes in 21 of 21 runs, and Haiku 4.5 in 18 of 21. It took six rounds; the first passed 3 of 10. Haiku's remaining misses are all things left out, which no check can catch; nothing wrong got through.

Answering questions about projects, search against the graph: eight questions such as “Who has to sign off the helpdesk contract?”, “Is anything blocked right now?” and “What was the status on 20 September?”, both models, two runs each, in folders holding the same files.

  • Told where the documents are (search at its best): the model querying the files got 29 of 32 right in 12.0 s on average; the graph got 32 of 32 in 4.5 s, 2.6 times faster.
  • Asked plainly: searching got 22 of 32 in 9.7 s, often answering “no information” without looking; the graph got 32 of 32 in 4.0 s.

Recall itself takes 2–10 ms.

What was built

PartFilesWhat it does
The record and its rulesprojects/model.pyA project as JSON: status history, owner, requester, due, dependencies, deliverables, plan, questions, decisions, sources. Ten operations change it, plus agree_plan from a discussion only. validate() refuses what an input does not say; apply() is pure; health() finds what needs a look.
Intakeprojects/ingest.py, ingest-prompt.mdOne Claude call turns a request, transcript or session write-up into a change set. The input is kept under inputs/, names are written in full, and a refused answer is sent back once with the problems. An input already dealt with is skipped.
Reviewprojects/review.py, commands/project-review.mdShow, apply or reject what waits in projects/pending/. Applying writes the record and its .log.jsonl history, notes the input, and rebuilds the graph and the board. The next session start says when updates wait.
The planprojects/plan.py, commands/project-plan.mdClaude researches (memory, then documents and code, read only, at most 20 calls), asks, drafts and waits for your agreement. plan.py saves the discussion as an input and records the plan: every step quotes a line you agreed. Findings are checked against their files and kept as sources.
Status in your wordsprojects/status.pyA status or step change with your reason saved and quoted; done or cancelled needs --yes.
Sessionsdigest/Each session write-up is kept on its own under inputs/sessions/ and offered to ingest; the changes wait for review. On by default ("projects": true).
The graphsrc/schema.sql, src/build_graph.pyProjects and deliverables are entities; status, due date, dependencies, decisions and open questions are claims dated from the day they were learnt; owners and dependencies are links. Steps, sources and history have tables. A record whose quotes are not in its inputs stops the build.
Answerssrc/recall.py, src/recall_hook.pyA project named by its title, an alias or a word of its title gets its status with the quote behind it and its plan in a line; the whole plan, deliverables or history when asked; a question naming no project reads the projects table.
The boardprojects/board.pyprojects/BOARD.md and one project's card.
Cases and evalsprojects/cases/, projects/eval_ingest.py, bench/project_eval.pySeven worked cases in one story, replayed by unit tests and re-tested live; eight live project questions.

The design, with the reasons for it, is in projects-proposal.html. How to use it is in projects/README.md.

The seven cases

Each case is an input, the projects before it, the change set a careful reader would propose, and the projects after it. They tell one story, in order: a kickoff meeting creates the helpdesk migration; a plan is agreed with you (through plan.py); later meetings, a typed request and a session write-up move it along and add a second project that waits on it. Unit tests replay every case without a model: the expected change set must pass the checks and apply to exactly the stored result. The live runs ask the model instead, and score the facts it must get right, not its wording.

Still open

  • Haiku leaves things out. The checks catch what is wrong, not what is missing. A completeness hint (a request's project with no requester) could trigger the repair round too.
  • The cases shaped the prompt. Six rounds tuned it against these seven cases, so the scores are likely optimistic. Cases written by someone who has not read the prompt would give a fairer number.
  • The plan discussion is tested without a model. plan.py and its checks are unit tested, and the discussion fixture renders byte for byte. The /project-plan conversation itself, with its research, has not been run live.
  • Nothing reminds anyone. Overdue and stale projects show on the board, in the build output and on their lines in the injection; nothing pushes them.
  • Two runs per question in the project eval, three in the ingest eval. Differences of a run or two are within noise; the search condition's times vary most.

python projects/eval_ingest.py --models sonnet,haiku --reps 3      # the model reads the cases
python bench/project_eval.py --models haiku,sonnet --reps 2        # the model answers project questions
